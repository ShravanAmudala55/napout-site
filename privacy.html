Privacy Policy

Effective September 30, 2026

In short: NapOut doesn't collect your personal data. There's no account, no ads and no tracking, and NapOut has no servers of its own. Your trips and settings stay on your phone. The app sends anonymous counts of how trips end, never where you go, and you can turn that off. If you turn on the music stream, songs come from Audius, which sees your IP address as any website would.

This policy explains how the NapOut app for iPhone (“NapOut”, “the app”), made by Shravan Amudala (“I”, “me”), handles your information.

What stays on your phone

To work, NapOut keeps a few things on your device only:

  • The stops you choose for a trip, and their map locations.
  • Places you save as favorites, with their map locations. Tap the star again to remove one.
  • While a trip is armed: when it was armed and its estimated travel time, so the alarm still works if the app is closed and reopened. This is cleared when the trip ends or you cancel it.
  • A history of trips you've finished: the two stops, when you set off and arrived, and how you travelled. You can delete single trips or all of them in Settings > Trip history.
  • Your settings, such as Quiet mode, wake distance and how you're travelling, the sleep sound you last played and whether you picked the sounds or the music stream, whether anonymous stats are on, and whether you've seen the welcome and tour.

None of this is sent to me. Deleting the app deletes all of it.

Location

NapOut uses your location only to do its job:

  • To fill in where you're boarding, when you tap Current location.
  • While a trip is armed, to see how close you are to your stop and wake you before you get there. If you allow Always access, iOS can wake NapOut near your stop even when the app is closed.

NapOut stops watching your location when the trip ends or is cancelled. Your location is never sent to me or to anyone else by NapOut, and you can change location access at any time in the iPhone Settings app.

Apple services

Maps, place search, directions and travel times come from Apple's MapKit. To show results, the text you search for and the places involved are sent to Apple, which handles them under Apple's Privacy Policy. Alarms are set through iOS's own alarm system.

Sleep sounds and music

The sleep sounds are built into the app: three are recordings stored inside it, and two are made on your phone as they play. Nothing is downloaded or sent for them.

The music stream is off unless you turn it on. If you do, NapOut fetches a list of calm songs from Audius, a music service for independent artists, and plays them over the internet. To do that, your phone connects to Audius and to the servers that host music on the Audius network, some of which are run by other companies. Like any website, they receive your device's IP address, which songs are requested, and that the requests come from NapOut. NapOut doesn't send them your location, your trips or anything else about you, and you don't need an Audius account. Audius handles this under its Privacy Policy.

If you tap Listen on Audius, the song's page opens in your browser, also under Audius's policy. Nothing is sent to Audius while you're listening to the sleep sounds.

Anonymous stats

To find out whether NapOut is waking people properly, the app sends a few anonymous counts to Aptabase, an analytics service built around privacy. They are:

  • When a trip is armed: bus or car, the wake distance, the trip's rough length in kilometres, and whether Quiet mode is on.
  • When the alarm rings: whether your location or the backup timer woke you, roughly how far from the stop and how long the trip was, and whether location and alarm access were allowed.
  • When a trip is cancelled, and when the music stream gives way to the sleep sounds.
  • With each of these: the app version, iOS version, iPhone model and language.

They never include your location, the names of your stops, your route or anything that says who you are, and there's no ID for you or your phone. To count how many people use the app in a day, Aptabase scrambles the IP address the counts arrive from together with a code it throws away every 24 hours, so nobody, me included, can follow a person from one day to the next. The counts are stored in the European Union. More in Aptabase's Privacy Policy.

You can turn this off in Settings > About > Anonymous stats. Off, nothing is kept or sent.

Sharing a trip

If you tap Share, NapOut prepares a short message about your trip. You choose whether to send it, which app to use and who receives it. NapOut doesn't see or keep what you share.

Crash reports

If you've chosen in iOS to share analytics with app developers, Apple may send me anonymous crash reports and basic usage statistics. They don't identify you, and you can turn this off in Settings > Privacy & Security > Analytics & Improvements.

Children

NapOut isn't directed at children under 13, and it doesn't collect personal information from anyone.

Changes to this policy

If NapOut's handling of information changes, I'll update this page and the date at the top.
